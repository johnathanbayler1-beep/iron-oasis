# Iron Oasis — Experience Bible

**Status: Pre-Production. Constitution document. No implementation authority overrides this document — implementation conforms to it, not the reverse.**

This is not a spec for a website. It is the direction for a short film that happens to be scrollable. Every decision below exists to protect one sentence:

> **"For the duration of your booking, this entire gym belongs to you."**

If a future decision — a section, an animation, a line of copy, a feature — does not serve that sentence, it does not belong in Iron Oasis.

---

## 1. What We Are Not Selling

- Not a membership. Membership is the mechanism, not the message.
- Not equipment. Equipment is scenery, not the subject.
- Not an app. The app is a key, not a product.

We are selling **exclusive, timed possession of a physical space.** Every scene, every word, every camera move is in service of making the visitor *feel* what it is to have a gym with nobody else in it.

---

## 2. The Visitor

Assume zero prior knowledge. No brand recognition, no context, no goodwill extended in advance. By the final scene they must understand, without ever being told directly in a bullet list:

1. What Iron Oasis is
2. Why it exists
3. Why it's different from every gym they've been to
4. How booking works
5. Where it is
6. Why privacy is the whole point, not a feature
7. Why they can trust it
8. What to do right now

The experience answers all eight through **narrative sequence**, not through a features section. If a scene can be replaced by a bullet point without loss, the scene has failed.

---

## 3. The Role of the Website

This is an interactive brand film with a scrollbar as the timeline. The visitor is not "browsing a page" — they are being walked through a space by a director who controls what they see, when they see it, and how long they're allowed to look.

Scroll position = timecode. Nothing scrolls "because content needs to go somewhere." Every scroll unit is a directed beat.

## 4. Motion Language — Principles Extracted from the Nate Herk Reference

We are not copying his visuals, his palette, or his layouts. We are extracting the *directorial grammar* and re-shooting it for a private gym in Windsor, Ontario.

### 4.1 Attention Management
One idea occupies the screen at a time. The eye is never given two competing focal points. Secondary information (labels, meta text, timestamps) is allowed only once the primary subject has been fully read by the eye — it appears *after*, small, quiet, in the periphery.

**Applied to Iron Oasis:** each scene has exactly one subject — a door, a rack, a phone, a keypad, a face. Never a hero image with three captions competing for attention.

### 4.2 Camera Choreography
The "camera" (scroll-scrubbed video/3D/parallax) behaves like it's mounted on a dolly or crane, not a slideshow. Movement has weight and follows a physical path — it approaches, it passes, it turns to reveal, it never simply "fades to next thing."

**Applied to Iron Oasis:** the camera physically walks the visitor from the street, to the door, into the space. Scenes 2–4 are one continuous camera move, not three separate cuts.

### 4.3 Scroll Rhythm
Fast reference work alternates dense, quick-scrub sequences with long holds where scroll velocity drops to near zero and a single frame is allowed to breathe. This is what prevents scroll fatigue — variation in pace, not constant motion.

**Applied to Iron Oasis:** Scenes 0–2 move briskly (arrival is fast, the world resolves quickly). Scene 4 (the private experience) is the longest hold in the entire film — this is the emotional thesis and it is not allowed to rush.

### 4.4 Pacing
Every scene has a clear in-breath and out-breath: build, peak, release. No scene ends by simply stopping — it hands off cleanly, like a cut on action in film editing.

**Applied to Iron Oasis:** transitions are written explicitly per scene below. None of them are "then it fades to the next section."

### 4.5 Negative Space
Void is not empty — it is tension held on purpose. Reference work spends enormous canvas real estate on black/near-black with a single small object, forcing the eye to work for meaning.

**Applied to Iron Oasis:** the Obsidian Matte canvas (`#050505`) already defined in `DESIGN.md` is not a background color, it is a storytelling tool. Emptiness = the absence of other people. The void *is* the privacy.

### 4.6 Cinematic Framing
Compositions favor asymmetry, rule-of-thirds, and off-center subjects with headroom that implies scale. Nothing is centered by default — centering is reserved as a deliberate emphasis device used sparingly.

**Applied to Iron Oasis:** the gym interior is always shown with implied scale beyond the frame — the visitor should sense a large space they don't fully see, because they'll be alone in all of it.

### 4.7 Layered Environments
Foreground, midground, background move at different rates (true parallax, not a single flat plane), giving the illusion of depth and physical presence rather than a flat scroll-jack.

**Applied to Iron Oasis:** equipment silhouettes, light shafts, and dust/particulate layers move independently to sell physical depth in the gym scenes.

### 4.8 Typography Timing
Text does not simply appear. It is choreographed to arrive on a beat, hold exactly as long as it takes to read once, and exit before it overstays. Type is treated as a character in the scene, not a caption bolted onto a video.

**Applied to Iron Oasis:** Syne (per `DESIGN.md`) is used for statements the visitor should feel, tracked-out uppercase micro-labels for the informational layer. Never both fighting for attention in the same beat.

### 4.9 Lighting Language
Light sources are motivated (a doorway, a spotlight, a screen glow) rather than ambient/flat. Light is used to direct the eye exactly where attention management (4.1) demands.

**Applied to Iron Oasis:** gold accent light (`#C9A84C`, per legacy palette — carried forward as the single warm accent against Obsidian Matte) always originates from a diegetic source: a keypad LED, a spotlight over a rack, a phone screen, sunrise through a window. Never a generic UI glow.

### 4.10 Annotation Philosophy
Reference work uses sparse, precise annotation — small callouts that clarify without cluttering, always secondary to the visual, always dismissible by scroll.

**Applied to Iron Oasis:** micro-labels (uppercase, tracked, small, per `DESIGN.md` typography rules) appear only to answer a question the visitor is already asking at that exact beat — never as pre-emptive marketing copy.

## 6. The Story, Scene by Scene

### Scene 0 — Loading
**Purpose:** Set the register before a single frame of the gym is shown. This is the curtain, not content.
**Emotion:** Anticipation, controlled stillness.
**Camera:** None — static black frame.
**Lighting:** A single point of gold light resolves from total black, like an eye adjusting.
**Environment:** Void. Obsidian Matte, no geometry yet.
**Typography:** None, or a single monospace/tracked timestamp-style counter — never a spinner, never a percentage bar that reads as "loading a webpage."
**Animation:** The point of light breathes (scale/opacity pulse) at a slow, deliberate rate — never a generic loading animation.
**Information revealed:** None yet. This scene's job is to lower the visitor's guard and slow their scroll instinct before Scene 1.
**Question answered:** None directly — but it primes "this is not a normal website" before anything is said.
**Transition:** The point of light expands and resolves directly into the logo mark of Scene 1 — no cut, no fade-to-white.

### Scene 1 — Logo
**Purpose:** Name the thing before showing it.
**Emotion:** Quiet confidence, not hype.
**Camera:** Locked, centered — the one deliberate use of dead-center framing (per 4.6, reserved for emphasis).
**Lighting:** The gold point-light from Scene 0 becomes the source that reveals the wordmark, as if a single spotlight found it in the dark.
**Environment:** Still void.
**Typography:** IRON OASIS in Syne, full weight, generous tracking. No tagline yet.
**Animation:** Letters resolve from the light source outward — not a fade-in, a *reveal*.
**Information revealed:** The name only.
**Question answered:** "What is this?" — partially. Name without context, deliberately withheld meaning.
**Transition:** The wordmark itself becomes a doorway — its negative space (the gap of the "O", or the space between words) is the aperture the camera pushes through into Scene 2.

### Scene 2 — Arrival
**Purpose:** Ground the visitor physically — this is a real place, not an abstraction.
**Emotion:** Curiosity, the feeling of pulling up somewhere at night.
**Camera:** Slow dolly-in on an exterior shot — street level, low light, one building.
**Lighting:** Motivated exterior lighting — streetlight, a single lit sign, warm gold glow leaking from inside.
**Environment:** Windsor, Ontario exterior. Unmarked, unglamorous street presence that makes the interior glow feel earned rather than staged.
**Typography:** A single tracked micro-label resolves in the corner: "WINDSOR, ONTARIO" — first concrete fact given to the visitor.
**Animation:** Continuous camera move (per 4.2) — this does not cut to Scene 3, it walks into it.
**Information revealed:** Location, and that this is a physical, specific, real place.
**Question answered:** "Where is this?"
**Transition:** Camera reaches the door; the door becomes the frame edge, and opening it is the cut into Scene 3.

### Scene 3 — Entering the Gym
**Purpose:** First reveal of the space itself — the "wow," but restrained, not a fireworks show.
**Emotion:** A held breath. The moment of stepping into somewhere unfamiliar.
**Camera:** First-person push through the doorway, continuing the Scene 2 dolly, now revealing depth (per 4.7 layered parallax — foreground door frame, midground equipment silhouettes, background depth into the space).
**Lighting:** Interior gold accent lighting switches on progressively as the camera advances, each fixture lighting a beat of the space in sequence — not all-at-once flood lighting.
**Environment:** Full interior — racks, negative space, implied scale beyond frame (per 4.6).
**Typography:** None yet, or a single word that lands once the camera stops moving: "EMPTY." — deliberately provocative, answered in the next scene.
**Animation:** Camera deceleration on arrival — this is the first true "hold" beat, signaling to the visitor's scroll hand that they should slow down.
**Information revealed:** What the physical gym looks like.
**Question answered:** "What is Iron Oasis, physically?"
**Transition:** The word "EMPTY." dissolves and is replaced, same position, by the truth: the space is empty *because it's yours* — hands off directly into Scene 4's thesis.

### Scene 4 — The Private Experience
**Purpose:** This is the emotional core of the entire film. Everything before this scene is setup; everything after is mechanism. This scene delivers the thesis sentence directly.
**Emotion:** Relief. Permission. The specific pleasure of a space with nobody else in it.
**Camera:** The longest hold in the film (per 4.3). Slow, near-static camera, allowing the visitor to simply look. Minimal movement — this is where scroll velocity should feel like it nearly stops.
**Lighting:** Warmest, most complete lighting state in the film — the gym fully lit, fully visible, fully *available*.
**Environment:** Wide, uninterrupted view of the full space with no other bodies in it. Emptiness is staged as luxury, not vacancy.
<br>**Typography:** The mission sentence itself, delivered directly and without hedge: *"For the duration of your booking, this entire gym belongs to you."* Set large, held long enough to be read twice.
**Animation:** Near-stillness. Any motion here is ambient only (dust in light, a fan blade, a subtle parallax breathing) — never anything that competes with the text.
**Information revealed:** The core promise, stated plainly for the only time in the entire experience.
**Question answered:** "Why is this different?" and "Why should I care?" simultaneously.
**Transition:** Once the sentence has been read, it recedes and a new question rises in its place: "How does this work?" — a direct verbal hand-off into Scene 5.

### Scene 5 — How It Works
**Purpose:** Convert the emotional promise into a concrete, trustworthy mechanism. This is where skepticism gets addressed.
**Emotion:** Reassurance, clarity, competence.
**Camera:** Shifts register deliberately — from cinematic dolly to a calmer, more procedural framing (the visitor understands they've moved from "feeling" to "understanding").
**Lighting:** Even, clean, slightly cooler than Scene 4 — this is the explanatory register, not the emotional peak.
**Environment:** Close, tactile shots — a booking action, a door unlocking, a private window of time made visible.
**Typography:** Short, sequential statements (not a numbered feature list dressed as narrative) — each one a single sentence, arriving on its own beat per 4.8.
**Animation:** Sequential reveal, one mechanism-beat at a time: book a window → the space is reserved as yours alone → arrive and it's already unlocked for you.
**Information revealed:** The booking model — solo windows, keyless access, exclusivity as a scheduling mechanism rather than a marketing claim.
**Question answered:** "How does booking actually work?"
**Transition:** The unlocking motion (a door, a keypad resolving green) carries directly into the device that performs that unlock — the phone — opening Scene 6.

### Scene 6 — The App
**Purpose:** Introduce the access mechanism without ever making the app the star. It is a key, not a product.
**Emotion:** Effortlessness.
**Camera:** Tight, intimate framing on a single hand and phone — the most human-scale shot in the film so far.
**Lighting:** Screen glow as the sole motivated light source (per 4.9), everything else falls to near-black around it.
**Environment:** Minimal — the phone and the door it's unlocking are the entire visible world in this beat.
**Typography:** Minimal, functional — this scene should feel the quietest in terms of type, because the *action* is the message, not a sentence about the action.
**Animation:** A single deliberate gesture (tap, unlock, door light shifts) — no interface tour, no screenshot carousel.
**Information revealed:** The app exists and is the mechanism of entry — nothing more.
**Question answered:** "What do I actually hold in my hand to get in?"
**Transition:** The unlocked door swings open onto daylight or street view, pulling the camera back out into the world for Scene 7.

### Scene 7 — The Location
**Purpose:** Reaffirm the physical, geographic reality of the place — closing the loop opened in Scene 2, now with enough trust built to give real specifics.
**Emotion:** Groundedness, credibility.
**Camera:** Pulls back to an establishing view — map-like or aerial, wider than anything shown before.
**Lighting:** Natural, daylight register — a deliberate contrast to the gold-lit interior scenes, signaling "this is the real world, not just the story."
**Environment:** Windsor, Ontario context — enough geographic specificity to feel real and findable, not a stock map pin.
**Typography:** Address-level specificity, tracked micro-label register consistent with Scene 2.
**Animation:** Minimal — this scene should feel like the camera setting down after a long flight, not another cinematic push.
**Information revealed:** Exact location, enough for the visitor to place this in their own life ("I could actually go there").
**Question answered:** "Where, precisely, is this, and could I get there?"
**Transition:** As the location settles, a quiet interface element appears at the frame edge — not intrusive, an invitation rather than a popup — introducing the concierge for Scene 8.

### Scene 8 — Ask Iron (AI Concierge)
**Purpose:** Offer a way to get personal, specific answers without breaking the film's authored pace for visitors who want to go deeper.
**Emotion:** Being personally attended to — the feeling of a concierge desk, not a support widget.
**Camera:** Softens — this is the one scene where the visitor may take the wheel, so the direction hands off gracefully rather than commanding further.
**Lighting:** Same gold accent register as the interior scenes — Ask Iron is diegetically *of* the gym, not a bolted-on SaaS chat widget.
**Environment:** A quiet, uncluttered surface — this is a threshold moment, not a new "page."
**Typography:** A single, warm, first-person invitation in Iron's voice (see Section 7) rather than a generic "Chat with us" prompt.
**Animation:** Understated entrance — Iron doesn't pop up aggressively; it arrives the way a concierge steps forward when you pause near a desk.
**Information revealed:** That personalized answers exist for whatever the film didn't cover.
**Question answered:** "What if I still have a question?"
**Transition:** Whether the visitor engages Iron or not, the natural next beat is resolved the same way — scrolling continues into the final scene, the ask.

### Scene 9 — Get Access
**Purpose:** The close. Convert everything felt and understood into one unambiguous action.
**Emotion:** Readiness. The decision should feel like the visitor's own idea by this point, not a sales push.
**Camera:** Returns to the Scene 4 register — warm, held, generous — closing the emotional loop of the film.
**Lighting:** Full gold-lit interior warmth, brightest and most complete lighting state alongside Scene 4.
**Environment:** A final wide shot of the space, empty, waiting — visually rhyming with Scene 4 to remind the visitor what they're being invited into.
**Typography:** One direct call to action, no qualifiers, no fine print in the hero beat itself.
**Animation:** A single, confident, final motion — the door, the light, or the booking action resolving to its "ready" state.
**Information revealed:** Exactly what to do next and nothing else competing for attention.
**Question answered:** "What do I do right now?"
**Transition:** None — this is the end of the directed film. What follows (footer, legal, secondary links) exists outside the narrative, deliberately visually demoted so it never competes with the close.

## 7. Ask Iron — The AI Concierge

Iron is not a chatbot bolted onto the site. Iron is **the digital concierge for the facility** — the same register as a well-trained front-desk presence at a private space, translated into text.

### Tone
Calm, precise, warm without being chatty. Confident the way someone is when they know a space intimately and enjoy showing it to you. Never salesy, never apologetic, never uses exclamation points as a crutch.

### Vocabulary
Complies fully with Section 5's brand vocabulary rules — Iron never says "workout," "gains," "grind," "sanctuary," "gym floor," or "clearance." Iron speaks in terms of *time*, *space*, and *access* — booking a window, holding a private node, arriving to an unlocked door.

### How It Greets Visitors
First-person, specific, low-pressure. Not "Hi! How can I help you today? 😊" — instead, something in the register of a person who has been expecting you: an opening line that acknowledges it's Iron, states plainly what it can help with, and stops. No forced enthusiasm, no emoji.

### What It Should Answer
- Booking mechanics and how solo windows work
- Pricing and access tiers, accurately, sourced from current published pricing (never invented)
- Location, hours of access, keyless entry mechanics
- What's in the space (equipment, layout) at a factual, non-hyped level
- Trust and privacy questions — what "yours alone" actually guarantees

### What It Should Never Do
- Never invent pricing, availability, or promises not backed by the actual booking system
- Never use banned vocabulary (Section 5)
- Never break character into generic customer-support-bot phrasing ("I'm just an AI...", "As a language model...")
- Never interrupt the directed scroll experience uninvited — Iron is opt-in, summoned at Scene 8's threshold, never a popup that hijacks earlier scenes
- Never oversell — if a visitor asks something outside Iron's certainty, it says so plainly and offers the human/booking path instead of guessing

### How It Fits the Story
Iron is introduced diegetically at Scene 8 as a natural extension of the concierge feeling the whole film has been building — lit the same way, spoken in the same voice as the rest of the copy. It is never presented as a separate "support" system bolted onto a marketing site; it is staffing the same private facility the visitor has just been walked through.

## 9. The Five Non-Negotiable Rules

These rules are permanent. Every future implementation, redesign, or feature addition must obey them without exception.

1. **The thesis sentence governs everything.** If a scene, feature, or line of copy doesn't serve *"For the duration of your booking, this entire gym belongs to you,"* it does not ship.

2. **One subject per scene.** No screen ever asks the eye to resolve two competing focal points at once (Section 4.1). If a scene needs two ideas, it needs to be two scenes.

3. **The banned vocabulary list is absolute.** "Clearance," "workout," "gains," "beast mode," "grind," "sanctuary," and "gym floor" never appear anywhere in Iron Oasis copy, in any tense, in any context — not in headlines, not in Ask Iron's responses, not in error states.

4. **Emptiness is the luxury, never the flaw.** Every depiction of the space with no other people in it must be staged and lit as an intentional privilege (Section 4.5), never allowed to read as a quiet, under-attended, or struggling business.

5. **Scroll pace must vary with intention.** No implementation may make every scene move at the same velocity. Fast establishing beats and long emotional holds (Section 4.3) are both required, and the single longest hold in the entire experience belongs to Scene 4 — nothing may be added later that steals that scene's pacing primacy.
